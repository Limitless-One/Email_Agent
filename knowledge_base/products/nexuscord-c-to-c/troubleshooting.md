# NexusCord 100W Braided Cable - Troubleshooting Guide

### Issue 1: The cable does not connect my laptop to an external monitor ("No Signal" error)
* **Root Cause**: The customer is attempting to use the cable as a video/display cable (DisplayPort Alt Mode).
* **Explanation & Resolution**:
  - The NexusCord 100W is specifically designed as a **high-amperage power charging cable** (with USB 2.0 480Mbps data transfer).
  - It does **not** contain the internal high-frequency twisted differential pairs required for DisplayPort Alt Mode, 4K/8K video transmission, or Thunderbolt 3/4 / USB4 video tunneling.
  - Inform the customer clearly that this cable is engineered for fast charging up to 100W, and recommend using a dedicated Thunderbolt 4 or HDMI/DisplayPort cable for external display monitor connections.
  - If the customer purchased the cable solely for monitor use, offer our standard 30-day hassle-free return or exchange.

### Issue 2: My device is charging slowly (e.g., "Slow charging" notification on phone or laptop)
* **Root Causes**:
  1. The wall charger adapter has a low wattage output (e.g., 5W, 10W, or standard USB-A to C adapter).
  2. The device port has debris or lint preventing full connector pin engagement.
  3. The wall charger does not support the specific fast charging standard of the phone (e.g., lacking PD or PPS).
* **Diagnostic Steps**:
  1. Check the wall power block: What wattage is printed on the wall charger? A 100W cable cannot deliver 100W if plugged into a 20W charger.
  2. Test with an alternate certified wall charger.
  3. Flip the USB-C connector over (reverse it 180°) and plug it back in.
  4. Inspect the charging port on the phone or laptop with a flashlight for compacted pocket lint. If present, gently clean it out using a non-conductive wooden or plastic toothpick.

### Issue 3: Intermittent charging (disconnects and reconnects when touched or moved)
* **Root Causes**:
  1. Accumulated debris inside the female port of the customer's device preventing the cable from clicking all the way in.
  2. Bent connector pin inside the cable or damaged internal trace due to excessive strain/crushing.
* **Diagnostic Steps**:
  1. Test the cable with a second device (e.g., a friend's phone or power bank).
     - If the cable disconnects across multiple devices when lightly touched, the internal conductor in the cable is fractured.
     - If the cable only disconnects on one specific device, the issue is almost certainly pocket lint inside that device's USB-C port preventing a firm mechanical latch.
* **Resolution**:
  - If verified defective across multiple devices and within the 1-year warranty period, issue an immediate warranty replacement (RMA).

---

### Issue 4: The cable fits loosely and easily falls out of the phone
* **Root Cause**: In 95% of cases, pocket lint is compacted at the bottom of the phone's USB-C port, preventing the connector from reaching its locking snap depth.
* **Resolution**:
  - Guide the customer to shine a bright light directly into the device port. The connector should seat with an audible/tactile "click" flush against the phone body. If there is a 1mm gap, compacted lint is preventing complete insertion.
